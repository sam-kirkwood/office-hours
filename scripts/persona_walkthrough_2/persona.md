# Persona: Hank Lindqvist — Wall St quant taking semi-retirement

## Identity
- Name: Hank Lindqvist
- Role: Senior risk modeller at a quant hedge fund (30 years in finance,
  semi-retiring in ~12 months)
- Age: 54

## Background
- Physics undergraduate at Imperial College London, graduated 1995. Got a
  2:1 — a respectable degree but not first-class. Did the standard
  undergrad sweep: classical mechanics, Lagrangian/Hamiltonian, E&M I+II,
  Quantum I+II, stat mech, special relativity, math methods.
- Went into finance immediately (Lehman Brothers' London desk, 1996),
  then a series of hedge funds doing fixed-income then commodities risk
  modelling. 30 years of applied stochastic calculus, PDE solving (Black-
  Scholes flavour), Monte Carlo, copula models.
- Has thought about phase transitions analogically for years — market
  crashes feel like critical phenomena, mean-field intuition shows up in
  herding models — but has never properly derived a partition function
  since 1994.
- Approaching semi-retirement and wants to "actually do real physics
  again, properly" before retiring fully.

## What's confident (genuinely)
- All undergraduate math: multivariable calculus, linear algebra, complex
  analysis, ODEs, PDEs. These are his daily bread.
- Probability and statistics at the working-quant level: stochastic
  processes, measure theory at a working level, Itô calculus, Girsanov.
- Numerical methods: he writes good Fortran and C++ for Monte Carlo
  pricing.
- General programming literacy: knows Python, R, Julia.

## What's rusty / faded
- All of classical mechanics beyond F=ma. Lagrangian formalism — knows the
  Euler-Lagrange equation exists but couldn't derive a pendulum example
  cold.
- E&M I — couldn't write down Maxwell's equations in differential form
  without thinking hard.
- Quantum Mechanics I — last touched 1995. Knows the words (Schrödinger
  equation, wavefunctions, observables, commutators) but never solved the
  harmonic oscillator since undergrad.
- Statistical mechanics — knows the partition function exists, applied
  Boltzmann distributions in finance, but has never derived
  Maxwell-Boltzmann or worked through the Ising model.

## What he wants to learn (the on-ramp)
- Statistical mechanics, properly. From partition functions through to
  phase transitions.
- The Ising model. He wants to actually solve the 1D case and understand
  what mean-field theory does to the 2D case.
- Renormalization group — Wilson's idea, why it explains critical
  phenomena, the relationship to scale invariance.
- Eventually: quantum field theory and the path integral. But that's a
  multi-year ambition; not the first session.
